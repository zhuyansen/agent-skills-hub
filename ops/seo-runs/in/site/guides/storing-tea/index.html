How to store loose leaf tea

Tea takes up moisture and smells from the air around it, and light and heat fade its flavour. Stored well, white tea keeps for years and even improves; green tea is best within six months of harvest.

The four rules

  • Airtight: keep the leaf in its resealable pouch, pressed flat, or in a tin with a tight lid.
  • Dark: a cupboard, not a glass jar on the counter.
  • Cool and dry: away from the kettle, the oven and the dishwasher. Do not keep tea in the fridge; it picks up damp when you take it out.
  • Away from smells: not next to spices, coffee or soap.

How long it keeps

Green tea is at its best for six months and drinkable for a year. White tea such as Bai Mudan changes slowly over several years, getting deeper and sweeter, which is why aged white tea is prized in Fujian. Jasmine tea keeps its scent for about a year; after that the jasmine fades before the tea does.

Every pouch we sell has the harvest date printed on the back, so you can tell how old your tea is.
