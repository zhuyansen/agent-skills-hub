How to brew green tea without the bitterness

Published 12 September 2026 by Mei Harding

Most bitter green tea is brewed with water that is too hot or left on the leaf too long. Green tea is not roasted or oxidised the way black tea is, so boiling water pulls out tannins and caffeine fast and cooks the delicate flavours. Get the temperature and the time right and nearly any decent green tea tastes sweet.

Water temperature

Brew most green teas at 75 to 80 °C. Without a thermometer kettle, boil the water and let it stand with the lid open for about four minutes, or add one part cold water to four parts boiling. Delicate Chinese greens such as Dragon Well, which we hope to stock next spring (see Dragon Well), like the lower end of that range.

How much leaf and how long

For a 250 ml mug, use 2 to 3 grams of leaf, about a heaped teaspoon, and steep for 2 minutes. For a teapot, scale up: 5 grams for 500 ml. In a gaiwan, use 4 grams for 100 ml and pour off after 30 seconds, adding 10 seconds for each infusion after that.

When the cup is wrong

  • Bitter: the water was too hot or the tea steeped too long. Cool the water or shorten the time, not both at once.
  • Thin or flat: too little leaf. Add more leaf before you lengthen the time.
  • Grassy and sharp: common with Japanese greens; try 70 °C.

The same temperatures work for white tea. Try them with our Bai Mudan, the most forgiving tea we sell.
