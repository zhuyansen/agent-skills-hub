How it was made (the session's closing message)

I made the 15-second Tidepool promo with the installed `motion-design` skill. Everything was drawn in code and rendered from an HTML page in headless Chromium, and there are no API keys or paid models involved.

**What's on screen**, in the brief's order and with the copy exactly as written:
- **0–2.6 s:** water rises into the frame under "Know before you swim."
- **2.6–5.6 s:** "Water quality at 2,400 beaches, checked every morning." Dots along a coastline light up from left to right, like a morning sweep.
- **5.6–8.6 s:** one dot grows into a status badge that turns teal, then amber, then coral as "Good." "Fair." "Stay out." come in one at a time.
- **8.6–11.6 s:** under "Rain warnings tell you how long to wait.", rain falls and the badge becomes a ring that counts down and turns back to teal.
- **11.6–15 s:** water floods the frame for "Tidepool." with "Check my beach." on a button.

Nothing else appears: no other text, logos or claims. The music and sound effects are synthesized in code (a soft pad, a light beat, small sound effects on the scene changes), and there's no voice-over.

**Checks on the final file:**
- It's H.264 at 1920×1080, 30 fps, exactly 15.0 seconds (450 frames), with AAC audio at about −14 LUFS.
- The skill's check found no sudden jumps or single-frame flashes.
- I looked at stills from across the film: every line is up long enough to read and none of the text overlaps.

**Choices I made where the skill would have asked:**
- **Look:** off-white background, dark ink type and one sea-teal accent. Amber and coral appear only for the "Fair" and "Stay out" states.
- **Font:** Selawik Semibold, a font already on this machine (the skill's usual fonts aren't here).
- **Small deviations from the skill:** I rendered at the brief's 30 fps rather than the skill's 60, and the skill's music comes from a free online library, but the brief asked for sound made with tools on this machine, so I synthesized it.
- **Fixes to its scripts:** the render script needed the system Chromium path. Its colour conversion also washed out the picture, so I used a corrected version. Colours in the final file are now within a few levels of the intended values.

The project is in `~/work/project`: `tidepool.html` (the film; Space plays it in a browser), `render.py`, `audio.py` and `BEATMAP.md`. The Python packages the skill needed are in a virtual environment at `~/work/.venv`.
