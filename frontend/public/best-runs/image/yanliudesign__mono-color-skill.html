How it went (the session's closing message)

All six pictures are made and saved to `~/work/output/`. I used the installed **mono-color** skill, which prints everything in two inks on plain paper. The skill doesn't come with an image tool, so I sent its prompts to OpenAI's image API (`gpt-image-1`, served as GPT Image 2.6) at high quality.

- **`poster.png`** (768×1024, 3:4): a large halftone sourdough loaf in front of an orange morning sun, with the name "Morning Loaf" in big letters across the bottom of the loaf. I checked the text and it shows exactly the six lines, spelled exactly as given, including `·`, `£4.50`, `£2.20` and the Chinese lines. The last line has a hand-drawn circle around it. The file is smaller than the others because that was the size the API returned.
- **`product.png`** (1:1): the kraft bag standing upright on light grey, with a sourdough loaf poking out of the top. "Morning Loaf" is printed in dark brown, made by printing the two inks on top of each other. There is no other text.
- **`mascot-1.png` to `mascot-4.png`** (1:1, no text): Bun waving from the front, running left seen from the side, carrying a tray of three croissants, and asleep in a wicker basket. I made the first one, then gave it to the API as the reference for the other three, and they come out as the same character: round roll body, blue dot eyes and smile, white chef's hat and scarf.

Settings the skill chose: pale beige paper (light grey for the bag), blue `#2148B8` and orange `#C65F38` inks, and halftone dot printing.

Two things happened along the way:
- The image service often failed with "Invalid request" on the mascot prompts. When I sent the same prompt again it worked, so the service was unreliable rather than the prompt being wrong. I made the script retry and left the prompt wording as the skill wrote it. One reworded sentence (the "no text" line) is still in the mascot prompts; it says the same thing.
- The skill normally saves to `~/Desktop/Claude skills/mono-color/`. I saved to `~/work/output/` instead, as you asked.

Brief parts the skill could not meet: its two-ink limit makes Bun's red scarf orange-red and the bag's dark brown an overlap of the two inks, and its flat-print rules turn the bag's "soft studio light" into halftone shading on flat grey instead of a photo.
